# Writing a voice that sounds like a person

Tonally hands the voice to an AI model as instructions for every reply. Whatever the voice
lists, the model reaches for - so a voice that is vague, or full of catchphrases, gives replies
that read as machine-written. These are the patterns that give a reply away, and how to keep them
out of the voice.

## Length and shape

Often the first giveaway is the shape: replies that are all long, all polished, all two tidy
sentences. Real replies are short and plain. What everyday replies on X look like:

| | Real replies |
| --- | --- |
| Median length | 26 characters |
| Under 40 characters | 70% |
| Two or more sentences | 10% |
| End with a full stop | 6% |
| Ask a question | 7% |

The same people write posts several times as long, so replies need their own rules. Numbers vary
by niche; treat these as a sense of scale, not as targets.

## Keep out of the voice

- **Catchphrase lists.** "the real bottleneck is", "nobody talks about", "plot twist", "in this
  economy". Listed as vocabulary, they turn up in nearly every reply. Keep `vocabulary` to a few
  plain words the person really uses (`yeah`, `depends`, `in practice`) - five at most.
- **Poster lines.** Tidy mirrored one-liners ("hire slow, fire fast") and "it's not X, it's Y" /
  "not X but Y". They are the clearest sign of AI writing.
- **Labels and praise as openers.** "Pro tip:", "Hot take:", "Underrated:", "Worth adding:",
  "Great point", "Love this", "Great question", or "This." as a whole reply.
- **Invented personal results.** "saved me months", "10x'd our revenue", "grew to 50k". The model
  would claim them in the user's name. Personal experience stays general: "we tried that", "most
  teams I have seen".
- **Stock words.** game-changer, delve, leverage, elevate, unlock, journey, crucial, "in today's
  world", "let's dive in".
- **Stock meme formats and habit emojis.** "nobody:", "the audacity", "me pretending", and 💀 😭
  out of habit.
- **Em dashes (—).** Tonally replaces them with a hyphen anyway; write a hyphen, a comma or a full
  stop.
- **Semicolons and tidy lists of three.** Each turns up in under 1% of real replies, and both read
  like an essay.

## Put into the voice

- **Rules a stranger could follow.** "one or two sentences, lowercase starts are fine, no full
  stop at the end" beats "casual".
- **A length mix, not one range.** "About half under 40 characters, the rest one sentence, longer
  only to explain something" - a single range like "80 to 200 characters" makes every reply the
  same size.
- **One sentence by default.** A second only when it adds a fact.
- **Full stops optional on short replies.** A voice that writes in sentence case can keep them on
  longer ones.
- **Uneven, natural rhythm.** Contractions, the odd fragment, sentences of different lengths.
- **A detail from the post.** Tell the voice to pick up something specific from the post it
  answers rather than state a general truth.
- **Different kinds of reply.** React to one detail, agree in a few words, add a fact, ask, joke,
  "same here" - and not the same kind twice in a row. Not every reply has to add an insight.
- **Questions only when they are real.** No "What do you think?" or "Thoughts?" tacked on at the
  end to get engagement - about 7% of real replies ask anything at all.
- **Varied openings.** Not every reply starts with "I", with the advice, or with the same word.

## Examples

Write 5 to 8 new example replies that show the rhythm. Vary length and opening. The AI copies the
examples more than the rules, so make 2 or 3 of them only a few words, and most of the rest one
sentence. Each must make sense as an answer to some post, under 280 characters. Links, @mentions
and hashtags only where the voice is meant to promote something - and then in some replies, not
every one.
